Mattress firmness for heavy sleepers (230 lb and up)

SomniSmart editorial team · Updated October 27, 2026

Most mattress testing and marketing is built around an average-weight sleeper. If you weigh 230 lb or more, the same mattress will feel softer, compress deeper and wear faster. This guide explains what changes, what evidence exists, and which specs to look for.

Why weight changes how a mattress feels

Firmness labels describe how a mattress feels to the brand's reference sleeper. A heavier body presses further into the comfort layers, so a mattress sold as “medium” can feel soft, and the heaviest part of the body, the hips, can sink below the shoulders. When the comfort layers are fully compressed, you are effectively lying on the support core, which is known as bottoming out. That combination can bend the spine and concentrate pressure, the opposite of what you want.

What the research says (and does not)

We could not find large trials that recruited heavier sleepers specifically, so the guidance below combines general mattress evidence with mechanics. Be wary of anyone claiming a study “proves” a specific mattress for heavier people.

  • Medium-firm still beats very firm in trials. In a randomized trial of 313 adults with chronic low-back pain, medium-firm mattresses led to better pain and disability outcomes than firm ones[1]. Reviews of the research found medium-firm and self-adjustable (custom-inflated) mattresses performed best for comfort, sleep quality and spinal alignment[2][3]. For a heavier sleeper, the target is a mattress that feels medium-firm under their weight, which usually means a firmer build on paper.
  • Support wears out where you lie. A study of springs taken from used mattresses found that coils from the weight-bearing centre were weaker than coils from the head and foot of the same mattresses, and that this weakness may not be visible when the bed is empty[4]. More weight means more load on those coils, so durability matters more.
  • Old beds can hurt. When people with minor sleep-related pain swapped beds averaging 9.5 years old for new medium-firm ones, their sleep quality improved and back discomfort fell[5].

Specs to look for

These are the guideline ranges the SomniSmart quiz uses when a sleeper (or either partner) is over 230 lb. They are rules of thumb from general sleep-product guidance, not research thresholds, and brands do not always publish every figure.

  • Feel: the quiz moves the target toward medium-firm or firm. Side sleepers still need some give at the shoulder, so they usually land at medium-firm.
  • Construction: a hybrid (foam or latex over pocketed coils) or latex build is usually more supportive and durable than all-foam at higher weights.
  • Coils: about 8 inches or taller, pocketed, with a lower (thicker) gauge of around 13–14 in the centre. Lower gauge means thicker, stiffer wire.
  • Comfort layers: roughly 3–4 inches in total, made of high-density materials, with a top-layer ILD around 19–30 for back or stomach sleepers.
  • Foam density: memory foam of 4 lb/ft³ or more and polyfoam of about 1.8–2.0 lb/ft³ or more. Density is the best single clue to how long foam will resist softening.
  • Edge support: a reinforced perimeter helps when you sit on the edge to get in and out of bed.
  • Foundation: check the brand's foundation and weight requirements so the base does not sag under the mattress.

Adjusting for sleep position

  • Back sleepers generally do well at medium-firm to firm under their weight. The lower back should feel supported, with no gap you can slide a hand into easily and no feeling of the hips sinking into a hammock.
  • Stomach sleepers need the firmest surface of all, because a sagging middle arches the lower back. A thin comfort layer over a strong core is usually best.
  • Side sleepers face the hardest trade-off: they need pressure relief at the shoulder and hip but strong support under the waist and hips. Zoned support (softer at the shoulders, firmer in the centre) and a thicker, high-density comfort layer help. Our side sleeper guide covers hip and shoulder pain in more detail.

Questions to ask a brand

  1. What is the density of every foam layer, not just the top one?
  2. What coil height, gauge and count are used, and is the centre zone reinforced?
  3. Is there a stated weight limit per side, and does the warranty cover body impressions at my weight?
  4. Is there a firmer version or a firmness exchange during the trial?

If a brand will not share foam densities, that is useful information in itself.

Couples with different weights

When one partner is over 230 lb and the other is much lighter, a single firmness often suits neither: it is too soft for one and too firm for the other. The SomniSmart quiz scores each person separately and tells you how far apart your ideal firmness levels are. When they differ by several points, a split king, a dual-firmness build or a mattress with separate support on each side is usually the better route.

Pillows and toppers

Heavier sleepers compress pillow fill more, so the pillow quiz starts about half an inch higher. A topper can soften a too-firm mattress, but it cannot fix a support core that has already sagged; if there is a visible dip, replace the mattress.
